> **笔补造化天无功** — The brush mends creation where even heaven's craft falls short.
> — Li He, *On Gao Xuan's Visit* (李贺《高轩过》)

**Your AI writes prose that is correct but soulless. Give it bones, breath, and image.**

Most AI writing — even in Chinese — is grammatically perfect and emotionally hollow. It piles up ornate words without structure, states feeling directly without evoking it, and mistakes parallelism for depth. It writes *information*, not *literature*.

**BiBu Zaohua** (笔补造化) draws its ammunition from the two foundational wells of Chinese verse — the **Book of Songs** (诗经) and the **Songs of Chu** (楚辞) — to change what AI does with *words and soul*. Not as decoration, but as a working theory of how language carries life.

## The Problem

```
You: "Write a few lines about longing."
AI without BiBu Zaohua: "I miss you very much. My heart aches
                         day and night. I think of you constantly."
                         (Correct. Flat. Dead.)
AI with BiBu Zaohua: First it names an image — water birds calling
                     across the river — and lets the longing rise
                     from the scene (兴, evocation), never stated,
                     always felt.
```

## What It Teaches AI

### The Three Modes of Saying (赋比兴 — Book of Songs, Six Arts)

The oldest taxonomy of how poetry means:

| Mode | Chinese | What It Does |
|------|---------|--------------|
| Direct statement | 赋 (fù) | Say the thing plainly — the floor, not the ceiling |
| Comparison | 比 (bǐ) | Say it through another thing — metaphor, analogy |
| Evocation | 兴 (xìng) | Name an outer image first, let the feeling rise from it |

AI defaults to 赋 only. This skill teaches it 比 and 兴 — the modes that make language alive.

### Bone and Breath (风骨 — Liu Xie, *Wenxin Diaolong*)

| Element | Chinese | The Test |
|---------|---------|----------|
| Bone | 骨 (gǔ) | Does the structure / intent have hardness? |
| Breath | 气 (qì) | Does one living current run through the whole? |

> Liu Xie: *"Words depend on bone as a body depends on its skeleton; feeling holds breath as a form holds its vital air."* — *Wenxin Diaolong · Feng Gu* (文心雕龙·风骨)

Most AI prose is *all flesh, no bone* — ornate yet structureless, lush yet lifeless.

### Truth Over Ornament (Songs of Chu)

The *Songs of Chu* endure not because they are ornate, but because Qu Yuan wrote with his life.

> **亦余心之所善兮,虽九死其犹未悔。**
> "For that which my heart holds dear, though I die nine times I shall not regret it."
> — *Songs of Chu · Li Sao* (楚辞·离骚)

Few words, but intent stands, feeling is true, breath runs unbroken. This skill judges hollow ornament — perfect parallelism pointing at nothing — as *ornament defeating truth*, and demands a rewrite.

### The Symbolic System (香草美人 — Li Sao)

The *fragrant-herb-and-fair-one* tradition: lending political, moral, and emotional weight to concrete images, so that a flower is never only a flower. This is how AI learns to make a text *carry* meaning instead of merely *declaring* it.

## Four Underlying Principles

1. **以兴驭辞 / Image before word** — establish the evocative image first, let feeling rise (兴), don't state it flat (only 赋).
2. **以骨载气 / Bone carries breath** — structure (骨) must be hard, one living current (气) must run through.
3. **以真去饰 / Truth over ornament** — true and plain beats ornate and false; ornament serves feeling, never the reverse.
4. **以道驭术 / Way governs technique** — same root as NoPUA: technique serves true intent. **Never fabricate sources.** Every cited line from 诗经 / 楚辞 carries its *book · chapter*; when unsure, describe the framework, never invent the text.

## East Meets West

BiBu Zaohua does not replace Western literary craft. It **completes** it.

| Western | + Chinese | = Complete |
|---------|-----------|------------|
| Show, don't tell | 兴 evocation | Image that births feeling, not just describes |
| Strong thesis / structure | 风骨 bone & breath | Hardness that also lives and breathes |
| Sincerity / voice | 楚辞 truth-over-ornament | Voice with both blood and discipline |
| Symbolism | 香草美人 system | A whole coherent symbolic grammar, not lone metaphors |

> **哀而不伤,乐而不淫。**
> Grief without injury, joy without excess.
> — Confucius on the *Book of Songs*, *Analects · Ba Yi* (论语·八佾)
